Asking books nothing and no payment is taken. We agree on the work in writing before you pay anything.

How it works.

  1. A free call. You tell me about your life, not about cars. The commute, the school run, what you carry, what you’d like to spend. “I don’t know yet” is a fine answer.
  2. We pick the car. I help you choose the model and the version of it that’s worth paying for.
  3. I work the deal. I ask dealers for itemized, out-the-door quotes on the car you want, compare them, and negotiate the price, the dealer fees and the add-ons. You see every number.
  4. You sign knowing the numbers. I check the final paperwork against the deal we agreed to before you sign. If the dealership is local, I’ll go with you.
The details

The $1,000 covers one new-car purchase: $500 to start and $500 when you buy. Payments already made for the same purchase count once toward it, including a $50 phone consultation.

You buy the car directly from the dealer. The car’s price, taxes and dealer fees are paid to the dealer, not to me, and no dealer pays me.

Going to the dealership with you is for local dealerships and depends on scheduling. Tell me where you’re shopping and we’ll work it out before you commit.

Before paid work starts, our written agreement confirms the included work, the timeframe, cancellation or refund arrangements and when each payment is due. See the service information.

Only want advice?

You don’t need a model, a budget or a dealer quote figured out. The first call is free: tell me what you’re trying to figure out, and we’ll see how I can help.

If you’d like to dig into your options without the full concierge, we can set up a 30-minute phone consultation for $50.

Ask about a free intro call

Already have a car in mind?

Two cars with the same model name can be equipped very differently. I help you pin down the version and the features you care about, then compare what’s available and the offers you have. A lower quote is less useful if it leaves out something you need.

Send the listings or window stickers you’re considering, your must-haves and your timing.

Already have a dealer quote?

Send it to me. I look at the vehicle price, dealer charges, trade-in and add-ons so you can see what each part contributes. For advertised rebates, I check who qualifies and whether the offer only applies with certain financing. The FTC explains why advertised discounts need that closer look.

The most useful quote is itemized, out-the-door and tied to one specific car. That means the purchase total before financing, including taxes and fees. The FTC recommends getting this in writing before visiting. If you don’t have one yet, I’ll tell you what to ask for.

For an add-on, the questions are what it costs, what it covers and whether you want it. Keep the quote and compare it with the final paperwork.

Financing or leasing?

A monthly payment matters to your budget, but it isn’t the whole cost. The amount financed, down payment, APR, loan length and total paid matter too. A longer loan can reduce the payment while increasing the interest cost. The CFPB’s loan comparison guide explains those tradeoffs.

I help you review the financing or lease terms in the offer and work out what to ask the dealer. You decide which terms work for you before committing.

Let’s talk about your next step.

Tell me where you are, even if that’s “I have no idea.” The first call is free, and the concierge is there when you want it handled.
